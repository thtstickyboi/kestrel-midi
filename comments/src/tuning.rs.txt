//! A tuning that gives every MIDI key a pitch of its own: `--edo N`, and Scala's
//! `.scl` and `.kbm` files (`--scala`, `--kbm`).
//!
//! **A table of 128 offsets.** A tuning is where each key plays, as an offset from
//! where 12-tone equal temperament puts it, in `mts::UNITS_PER_CENT` (1/2048 of a
//! cent, the unit the MIDI Tuning Standard messages are held in). The offset is what
//! the driver adds to a voice's pitch, and it also says which sample to use: the
//! region is chosen by the key *nearest the pitch*, not by the key that was struck,
//! so a 31-EDO key plays the sample recorded closest to its real pitch, transposed by
//! at most half a semitone, instead of its 12-tone key's own sample stretched many
//! semitones (`Driver::tune_note`). With no tuning there is no table and nothing is
//! changed: every reference render is the same bytes.
//!
//! **What stays on the key:** note-offs, the gate, keyswitches, the analytic phase
//! angle and every per-note identity. A tuning changes what a key sounds like, never
//! which note it is. Drum parts are not tuned.
//!
//! **Scala.** The `.scl` file is the scale: a description, a count, and that many
//! pitches in cents (a number with a `.`) or as ratios (`3/2`, or a whole number),
//! the last being the period, usually the octave. The `.kbm` is the keyboard map
//! that says which key plays which degree and where the reference note is; without
//! one the keys follow the scale in order from key 60, which plays the reference
//! pitch. Every error names the file's line.
