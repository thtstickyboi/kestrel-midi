//! Key-major merlin, step 2: one window, the static case, on the CPU.
//!
//! THE PLAN's build order (the dev notes, "Proposed: key-major rendering"), step
//! 2. Time-major merlin steps through the song a block at a time with every
//! waveform live; this renders one group's notes for the whole song at a time
//! and adds the groups into one master in a fixed order.
//!
//! **This is the reference implementation, not the fast one.** It resolves
//! every note the bucket pass filed exactly as `MerlinSynth::plan_note` does
//! -- release bucket, reference point, kernel segment and depth, impulse gain,
//! head record -- and then, where time-major has a frequency-domain delay line
//! and fixed-point scatters, it adds each entry's kernel at its impulses
//! directly and plays each head correction the way `head.wgsl` does, in f64.
//! What it is for:
//!
//! - its own gate, a null against time-major merlin with controllers pinned,
//!   which proves the decomposition and the bucket store end to end before any
//!   of it moves to the device;
//! - then the oracle the GPU version is nulled against, the way `cpu.rs` is for
//!   the per-voice backend.
//!
//! ## Groups are (key, filter), not keys
//!
//! A note reaches the output through its velocity's biquad (`bus.wgsl`; the
//! reference bank is 128 velocity layers that differ only in cutoff). Kernels
//! are filterless and shared across filters, but a group's notes can only be
//! summed before the biquad if they share it. So a key is split by filter, each
//! (key, filter) group is filtered from rest over its own extent -- exact, for
//! the reason `bus.wgsl` gives: the biquad is linear and every contribution is
//! zero before the group's first frame -- and the master is their sum in key
//! order, then filter order.
//!
//! ## Where it differs from time-major on purpose
//!
//! - No note-length ceiling and no head-cache clamp.
//! - No emit lag: a head plays from the note-on, and an impulse is never moved
//!   later because its block has gone out (`impulses_late`).
//! - A note's params come from the bucket pass's capture at the note-on, where
//!   time-major reads its variant slot at the note-off (see THE PLAN, "Step 1").
//! - No fixed point and no partitioned FFT, so none of their rounding.
