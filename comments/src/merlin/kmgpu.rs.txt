//! Key-major merlin on the device: a window of the song at a time, every key's
//! groups convolved into per-filter buses, their heads gathered onto them, the
//! buses filtered and mixed, and only the window's master read back. The
//! passes are `shaders/kmconv.wgsl`.
//!
//! ## The loop
//!
//! [`KmGpu::begin`] sizes the buses. For each window, [`KmGpu::key`] takes one
//! key's kernels, its groups' impulses and its head pieces, in key order, and
//! [`KmGpu::end_window`] runs the biquads, mixes and hands the window back.
//! Everything a key adds to a bus lands in key order and, within a key, in a
//! fixed order -- the convolution chunk by chunk, then the heads gathered page
//! by page -- so the bytes are a pure function of the input.
//!
//! ## Buses, as time-major has them
//!
//! A note reaches the output through its velocity's biquad, so the groups of a
//! key are by filter and the buses are one per filter the key uses,
//! `[filters][window + carry]` frames of `left, right`. The biquad then runs once per bus over the
//! song, serially as a biquad must, its state carried from window to window --
//! which is cheap because a bus is one song long, where filtering each (key,
//! filter) group on its own runs the song once per group (9,813 groups on the
//! 44.75 M-note file, 2026-10-22).
//!
//! A block's tail runs up to `n` frames past its window, into the carry, which
//! the next window starts with. A head is clipped to its window
//! (`HeadNote::clip`), so it needs no carry.
//!
//! ## The transform
//!
//! `merlin::fft::Fft` does one transform per workgroup and so stops at 2048
//! points. The large one is the four-step: `n = N1 * n2`, a batched
//! `n2`-point transform down the columns, a twiddle, a batched `N1`-point
//! transform along the rows, with tiled transposes between
//! (`examples/conv_bench.rs` measured it and checked it against a direct
//! convolution). The twiddles come from an f64 table rounded once, as
//! `fft.rs`'s do. The spectrum stays in the four-step's own order: the
//! kernels' are in the same order and the inverse undoes it.
//!
//! Every transform goes through `Fft::encode_exact`, whose batch is the
//! dispatch size, so a chunk can hold transforms of several batch counts in
//! one submit -- `Fft::encode`'s per-direction uniform would give them all the
//! last count written.
