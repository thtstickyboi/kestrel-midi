//!
//! the development notes section 5 makes this the largest risk in the convolution

[18] src/merlin/fft.rs:440
/// Persistent buffers for forward-transforming many rows in one dispatch and
/// **leaving the spectra on the device**.
///
/// This is how the kernel cache is built. Each kernel is `depth` partitions of
/// `2n` reals, and every kernel a partition step is missing is packed one after
/// another into `data`, transformed by one dispatch, and left in `spec` for the
/// caller to copy into the cache pages with `copy_buffer_to_buffer`. Nothing
/// comes back to the host: `r2c_once` created two buffers, submitted, and
/// blocked on a readback per kernel, and the spectra then went straight back
/// to the device through `write_buffer`.
///
/// **Bit-identical to transforming each row alone**, and for a reason rather
/// than a test: `shaders/fft.wgsl` runs one workgroup per row, every
/// workgroup reads only its own row of `data` and the shared twiddle table and
/// writes only its own row of `spec`, and its stage schedule is a function of
/// `n` alone. The batch count appears nowhere in the arithmetic. The test is
/// `tests/merlin_fft.rs::a_batch_gives_each_row_the_spectrum_it_has_alone`.
///
/// Both buffers **grow on demand** by doubling, up to `max_rows`, and are
/// never shrunk: the render's peak step sets them and the common step needs a
/// fraction. `max_rows` is what the caller may stage in one go, so a step that
/// needs more goes up as several batches.
