//! Merlin as a [`Backend`], so the existing driver can render through it.
//!
//! The pieces all exist by now -- the FFT, the FDL, the kernel builder, the
//! planner, the horizon. This is what wires them to the trait `cpu.rs` and
//! `gpu/` already implement, and it is where the one structural mismatch has to
//! be dealt with.
//!
//! ## The trait is push-per-block; Merlin needs to look ahead
//!
//! `spawn` hands over a block's note-ons and `render` immediately asks for that
//! block's audio. But the impulse sits at the note-*on* frame and carries
//! `r^(Dj-D)`, so Merlin cannot place it until the note-*off* has been seen --
//! and that arrives in a later `set_gates`.
//!
//! So **`render` returns the block `latency_blocks` behind the one just
//! spawned**, and the driver compensates by running that lead off the front --
//! [`Backend::output_latency_frames`]. Its existing tail handling covers the
//! other end, because [`Backend::stats`] reports work outstanding until the
//! buffer drains.
//!
//! The lag is set by the **release bucket width**, not by the note length: an
//! impulse sits at the note's release reference `Dj`, which is the bucket's
//! start and so at most one width before the note-off that resolves it.
//!
//! ## A long note is not held back; it is emitted while it is still open
//!
//! Waiting for the note-off would mean waiting for a note that may run sixteen
//! seconds, and the blocks it belongs in would be long gone. It does not have
//! to: a note that has not been released is still sustaining, so what it owes
//! the output is `A[n]` -- no release, no `r^(Dj-D)`, nothing that depends on
//! `D`. `render` emits that block by block from the head cache while the note
//! is open, and `plan_note` picks up from where that left off when the note-off
//! finally arrives. `HeadNote::from` is the seam.
//!
//! *Nothing did this before, and the impulse sat at the note-**on** rather than
//! at `Dj`, so a note outliving one block lost its delay-line contribution
//! outright and kept only whatever of its head fell after its own note-off. On
//! material whose mean note is 1.11 ms that is rare. One test file has 533,063
//! notes over half a second.*
//!
//! ## Channel controllers, and why they are resolved at the note-on
//!
//! the development notes section 6 sorts them into four classes. Three of them are
//! here:
//!
//! * **Folds into the note's gain** -- CC7 volume, CC11 expression and CC10 pan
//!   are read from the channel row current at the note's *own* frame and
//!   multiplied into `gain_l`/`gain_r`. From there they ride the impulse and
//!   the head correction, both of which already carry a per-note gain, so this
//!   costs nothing.
//! * **Widens the cache key** -- channel bend scales the note's 32.32 step, and
//!   the step is already in [`WaveKey`], so a bent note is simply a different
//!   waveform. CC71-CC75 arrive as a params variant, which
//!   [`Backend::set_params_variant`] now stores and which reaches the kernel
//!   through [`Kernel::from_cmd`]'s params table.
//! * **Falls back to the per-voice backend** -- swept bend, portamento, legato.
//!   Not implemented; merlin renders swept bend as the bend at the note-on.
//!
//! The one class that is *not* here is section 6's second row, **the
//! per-channel bus**, which would make channel gain and pan sample-accurate for
//! a note still ringing when its channel moves. Merlin's bus is already the
//! per-velocity biquad of section 3(c), so a per-channel bus is a cross
//! product: sixteen times the ring (752 MiB becomes 12 GiB at the bus ceiling),
//! and sixteen times the *firing rows*, which is the one quantity the whole
//! cost model is about. Section 6 lists both rows as legitimate; merlin takes
//! the free one. What it costs is stated in the design's handout.
//!
//! ## What is not here yet
//!
//! Residency scheduling (every entry stays resident), the per-voice fallback
//! for swept bend, and CC120's hard cut -- a channel silenced by All Sound Off
//! releases normally here instead of being damped over the steal window.

/// Prices the design's option 2 for CC7 without changing a sample.
///
/// **What option 2 is.** A released kernel is a pure exponential, so truncating
/// it at `t_j` and restarting is exact up to `r^(t_j - f)`. That makes a step
/// in channel gain correctable by one extra impulse -- and crucially by one per
/// `(entry, channel)` rather than one per note, because every note of an entry
/// on a channel shares the kernel and their combined tail is
/// `SUM_i g_i * kernel(t - t_i)`. One running scalar per pair carries it.
///
/// **What is unknown is the count**, and it is the whole question: an impulse
/// has to be fired for every pair that is still live when its channel's gain
/// moves, so the cost is `live pairs on moving channels`, per gain change. This
/// counts exactly that against a real render.
///
/// A pair is live from the step its entry fires until `depth` steps later,
/// which is the kernel's own length -- past that the tail has left the ring.
/// Expiry is bucketed rather than scanned, so this is O(1) a step and the probe
/// does not distort the timing it is measuring.
