//! The GPU self-test: how long this GPU takes over one block, at voice counts
//! that double until the next would risk taking too long.
//!
//! Windows resets a GPU that spends more than 2 s on one piece of work, and
//! Kestrel sends it one block at a time. Two users lost the GPU that way on
//! 2026-09-25 and 26, and neither could say how long their card took. This
//! measures it, and turns it into a plain answer: how many voices this card
//! can take at the default block before a block nears the limit.
//!
//! **What it renders.** A synthetic worst case: every voice sounding at once,
//! looped, reading a 128 MiB sample pool, which is too big to sit in cache,
//! as a real piano library is. It starts at 16,384 voices and doubles. It
//! stops once a block takes over [`STOP_MS`], so the next step, twice the
//! voices, stays near 0.5 s. The test itself never comes near the 2 s limit.
//!
//! **It runs in a process of its own.** The machine report is what a user sends
//! when their GPU misbehaves, and the self-test is the one part of it that
//! drives the GPU, so it is the part likeliest to meet a driver that crashes.
//! Run inside the report's own process, that took the whole report with it: on
//! 2026-10-05 a user's integrated GPU ended Kestrel with an access
//! violation (0xC0000005) at the Dx12 test, and no report was written. So the
//! report starts `kestrel falconeye-selftest` for each adapter and reads what it
//! prints, a line a step; if that process crashes, hangs or fails, the report
//! says how and at how many voices, and goes on to the next adapter. See
//! [`run_isolated`].
