//! How many voices one submission to the device covers.
//!
//! Windows resets the graphics driver when one piece of GPU work runs past
//! 2 s, and on NVIDIA's Vulkan driver the process is then ended outright
//! (a mid-range card, 15M voices, 2026-10-03). A
//! block that covers more voices than the budget goes up as several
//! submissions, so no one of them runs long. Where the work is cut changes no
//! output bit, which is what lets the budget move with the machine.
//!
//! **It starts at `Config::submit_voices` and only ever comes down on
//! evidence.** The default is over three times the default pool, so a stock
//! render never splits at all and the splitting costs it nothing. A part that took the
//! host over [`SLOW_SECS`] to wait for halves the budget, and a run of
//! [`CALM_BLOCKS`] blocks whose full-size parts were all short doubles it back.
//! Each extra submission costs about 0.7 ms of idle device, so a part worth
//! half a second is a rounding error against it, which is why parts are sized
//! by time and not made small to be safe: the 2026-09-26 build used a fixed
//! 262,144 voices and cost a dense render 4.5%.

/// The host waited this long, per part, for the device: halve the budget.
///
/// The wait is what is left of the device's time after the host's own work
/// for the next block, so it understates the device and never overstates it.
/// That is the right way round for a trigger: a host-bound render, whose
/// device sits idle, can never be told it is slow. The limit is 2 s, and the
/// parts of a block are not equal (the first carries the steal and spawn, the
/// last the reduce and compaction), so the trigger leaves room for the largest
/// being a good deal over the average.
