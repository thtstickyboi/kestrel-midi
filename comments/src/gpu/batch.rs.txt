//! Several renders in one dispatch: the stems runner's way past the device's
//! per-block floor.
//!
//! **Why.** A block that sounds costs about 1.6 ms of render pass whatever its
//! voice count -- one invocation per voice walks the block's frames and the
//! workgroup reduces every tile -- so a thin stem keeps one or two workgroups
//! busy and leaves the rest of the card idle. Separate renders cannot fill it:
//! one queue's pipeline barriers order every render's passes after every
//! other's. Measured 2026-09-23, 34 stems: 19.84 s at one job, 15.25 s at
//! eight. See the development notes, phase 3.
//!
//! **How.** A batch holds `lanes` renders. Every buffer a render owns is one
//! buffer here with a region per lane, and every pass is dispatched once for
//! the whole batch with the lanes along the grid's second axis. The shaders
//! are not written twice: [`laned`] rewrites the solo ones as the pipelines are
//! built, adding the lane's base to every access of a lane's own buffer and
//! reading the lane's uniforms from an array. The sample pool and the
//! read-only tables stay shared.
//!
//! **Why the result is the same bytes as a solo render.** Nothing a lane
//! computes depends on the other lanes or on how wide the batch's grid is:
//! the render pass assigns voices to workgroups by `u.render_workgroups`, the
//! reduce reads partials by it, and every other pass either works per item or
//! per block of items or counts with integer atomics. A lane's region holds
//! exactly what its solo render's buffers would. So a stem rendered in a batch
//! is the bytes `--track` writes for that track, which is what the tests check.
